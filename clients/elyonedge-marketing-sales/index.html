The problem

Schools rarely lose admissions loudly. They lose them quietly.

A parent messages the Facebook page and waits two days for a reply. A phone enquiry is answered, but no one follows up after the call. A tour happens — and then nothing. None of these moments feel like failure at the time. Added together over a term, they are the difference between a full class and a half-empty one.

We call them loopholes: predictable points where a genuinely interested parent falls out of the process, not because they changed their mind, but because no one built a system to hold their interest.

  1. Lead capture gaps

    Enquiries arrive by call, WhatsApp, Facebook and walk-in, and are never consolidated — so nobody can say with confidence how many the school actually received last month.

  2. Undifferentiated messaging

    The same generic pitch goes to every parent, whether they are weighing academics, nurturing, cost or location — so it resonates fully with none of them.

  3. A missing qualification step

    Tours are booked the moment they are requested, with no short conversation first to understand the parent’s real decision criteria — wasting tour slots and the chance to tailor the visit.

  4. Generic materials instead of tailored answers

    A standard prospectus is handed over where a specific answer to the parent’s actual question would have moved them further. Information without persuasion.

  5. No pipeline visibility

    No one can say, on any given day, how many enquiries are sitting at each stage — so warm leads go cold simply because nobody was watching.

The ElyonEdge Conversion Framework

Six stages every admissions process moves through — whether or not anyone has written them down.

Naming them is the first step to managing them. Here is what “good” looks like at each stage, and the loophole we find there most often.

Stage 01

Enquiry

Every enquiry — call, WhatsApp, Facebook, walk-in — is logged in one place the same day it arrives.

Common loopholeEnquiries live in someone’s head or a personal phone, not a shared, trackable record.

Stage 02

Qualified

A short conversation establishes the child’s age and grade, the timeline, and what the parent actually cares about.

Common loopholeTours are offered before anyone understands what the parent is deciding between.

Stage 03

Tour booked

A specific date and time is confirmed within 24–48 hours of the enquiry, while interest is highest.

Common loophole“We’ll get back to you” replaces a firm booking, and momentum is lost.

Stage 04

Toured

The visit answers the parent’s specific concerns rather than running a generic script, and ends with a clear next step.

Common loopholeA warm tour ends with “let us know if you have questions” instead of a defined follow-up.

Stage 05

Application

A follow-up happens within 48 hours of the tour, addressing anything left unresolved.

Common loopholeNo one follows up after the tour unless the parent chases the school first.

Stage 06

Enrolled

The admission is confirmed, and the parent is asked for a referral or testimonial once settled.

Common loopholeThe relationship goes quiet the moment payment is received.
